1. Spatial variational identities: independently verified
---------------------------------------------------------

Integrate P D_t^a eta by parts in t:

  P D_t^a eta = (-D_t)^a P eta
                + D_t sum_{b=0}^{a-1} (-D_t)^b P D_t^{a-1-b} eta.

Apply this separately to every L_{phi_{a,k}} D_t^a D_x^k eta,
then integrate the resulting F_k D_x^k eta in x. This gives precisely
(2.1)--(2.3). Setting eta=phi_t or phi_x gives

  D_t Tcal^0_nu + D_x Tcal^1_nu = -E(L) D_nu phi.

For the spatial Legendre transform, variation of Z cancels because
pi_{K-1}=L_{phi_{0,K}}. Therefore

  a_m=Phi_{m+1} (m<K-1), a_{K-1}=Z,
  b_0=-F_0, b_m=pi_{m-1}-F_m (m>=1).

The recursion pi_{m-1}=F_m-D_x pi_m gives (2.7), including the minus
sign on E(L).

Verdict: Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2 are correct in the stated local/formal setting.
The current-uniqueness assertion uses local horizontal exactness; it is
not a global topological statement.

2. Tangency and the two sewing equations: independently verified
----------------------------------------------------------------

Write h=H_-(Phi,Pi^-)-H_+(Psi,Pi^+). First integrate by parts all
derivatives of C in the balance, so the representative is

  h = rho . C + D_t B.

This choice of rho is needed; a coefficient before integrating by parts
the derivatives of C is not in general the correct multiplier.

The variational derivatives of h on C=0 are

  E_Phi h = b_- - S_PhiPhi a_- - S_PhiPsi a_+,
  E_Psi h = -b_+ - S_PsiPhi a_- - S_PsiPsi a_+,
  E_lambda h = -S_lambdaPhi a_- - S_lambdaPsi a_+.

The derivatives of rho.C reduce on the prolonged constraint locus to
S_Philambda rho, S_Psilambda rho, and S_lambdalambda rho. This gives
(3.5) with its printed signs. Substitution into (3.2) yields
delta pi_-=-b_- and delta pi_+=-b_+.

For a promoted solution, subtract the lift of (a_-,a_+,rho) from
D_x Y. The configuration components vanish. Consequently

  -E_- delta_{m0} = -S_Phi_m,lambda kappa,
  -E_+ delta_{m0} =  S_Psi_m,lambda kappa,
  0 = S_lambdalambda kappa.

This proves (3.4), and (I) gives the claimed bulk equations. For Lemma
3.5, the remaining difference has components (0,eta,kappa); (T)
annihilates it. The right Hamilton equations then recover the right
Ostrogradsky lift and its Euler--Lagrange equation.

Verdict: Theorem 3.3 and Lemma 3.5 are correct under their printed
injectivity hypotheses and the strong off-shell balance. This does not
prove their hypotheses from ordinary on-solution momentum conservation.

3. Lemma 3.2: the converse from physical conservation is false
-------------------------------------------------------------

Lines 130--140 equate physical conservation on all allowed boundary
jets with membership in the differential ideal generated by C.
For singular constraints, vanishing on C=0 is weaker than membership.

Take the same admissible bulk on both sides:

  L(w)=1/2 w_t^2 - 1/2 w_x^2 - 1/2 m^2 w^2, m>0,

and let

  L_d=lambda (psi-phi)^2.

Put d=psi-phi. Direct variation gives

  C=d^2,
  Pi^-=2 lambda d, Pi^+=2 lambda d.

Over real fields, C=0 implies psi=phi, their t derivatives agree,
and both spatial momenta vanish. Therefore H_-=H_+ on every allowed
boundary jet. Physical total momentum is conserved with P_d=0.
The boundary history phi(t)=psi(t) is otherwise free as spatial Cauchy
data for the two bulk equations, subject to the Neumann sewing data.

However, before imposing C=0,

  h=H_--H_+
   =1/2(psi_t^2-phi_t^2)+1/2 m^2(phi^2-psi^2).

Thus

  E_Phi h = phi_tt+m^2 phi,
  E_Psi h = -psi_tt-m^2 psi,

which do not vanish on the prolonged diagonal d=0. If
h=D_t B+rho C, its Euler derivative on that diagonal would be
D_C^* rho=0, since D_C=(-2d,2d,0). This is a contradiction.
All differential-ideal terms reduce to rho C modulo D_t by integration
by parts, so allowing derivatives of C does not remove the obstruction.

Verdict: wrong as an equivalence. Definition 3.1 is a strong sufficient
off-shell condition, and the paper may choose to work with it. It is
not automatically the "precise form" of on-solution conservation for
arbitrary multiplier constraints.

Suggested replacement at lines 136--140:

  "Condition (3.3) implies conservation of the modified momentum.
  Conversely, on-solution conservation implies (3.3) when the local
  vanishing ideal of the prolonged constraint locus is generated by C
  and its total t derivatives. Without this regularity assumption we
  impose (3.3) as a stronger off-shell hypothesis."

4. The abstract drops the essential condition (I)
------------------------------------------------

Use the same massive bulk and

  L_d=lambda(psi+phi).

The sewing equations are

  psi=-phi, phi_x=lambda, psi_x=-lambda.

The momentum balance holds on the constraint because the potential is
even and the two spatial Hamiltonian densities agree. But

  phi=x^2, psi=-x^2, lambda=2x

satisfies every promoted sewing equation and has

  E_-[phi]=2-m^2 x^2,
  E_+[psi]=-2+m^2 x^2.

Neither is identically zero. Here S_lambdalambda=0, so (I) fails.
This is exactly why Theorem 3.3 contains (I).

Verdict: the unconditional "Consequently every solution..." sentence
at line 9 is wrong. Theorem 3.3 itself survives.

Suggested replacement at line 9:

  "Under the injectivity condition (I), every solution of the sewing
  equations imposed at all points solves both bulk equations."

Calling psi=-phi a symmetry Baecklund relation does not make every
unrestricted promoted triple a pair of bulk solutions.

5. Noether charges and closure: algebra independently verified
-------------------------------------------------------------

At the defect, substituting the sewing momenta into the difference of
Noether fluxes gives

  J_--J_+ = E_Phi(L_d).Q_Phi + E_Psi(L_d).Q_Psi
          = pr(v_Q)L_d-C.Q_lambda-D_t Xi_d(Q).

Under the stated invariance hypothesis this is
D_t(A_d-Xi_d(Q)) modulo C. This proves Lemma 4.1's main assertion.
For N=D_x nu, integration over the two half-lines gives the boundary
contribution nu_--nu_+, cancelled by nu_+-nu_-.

For Proposition 4.2, direct differentiation gives

  D_t [D_x Q_d+Delta N]
     = D_x Delta J+D_t Delta N=0.

In setting B the stated limit fixes the integration constant. In setting
A one needs an applicable theorem that ker D_t contains only constants;
the printed invocation of Lemma 6.3 does not establish this for all
claimed cases (see item 7 below).

For Proposition 4.3, subtract (4.2) between the two sides:

  D_t Q^1 = Delta J_1
          = -Delta N_2-D_t Delta a_0-D_x Delta b_0
          = D_x Q^2-D_t Delta a_0-D_x Delta b_0.

This proves (4.3), once the potentials in Proposition 4.2 are available.

For Lemma 5.2, expansion gives

  x(D_t a_1+D_x b_1)+t(D_t a_2+D_x b_2)
  +a_2+b_1+D_t a_0+D_x b_0=0.

Since the autonomous equation leaves x,t independent of the fibre jets,
each coefficient vanishes. The general affine-current claim is correct.

Checks of four displayed closure identities:

  Relativistic: N_2=-T^01, J_1=T^10, and T^10=T^01.

  NLS: N_1=u bar(u),
       J_1=i(u bar(u)_x-u_x bar(u))=2p, N_2=-2p.

  KdV: J_1=-3u^2+u_xx, N_2=3u^2, b_0=-u_x;
       N_2+J_1+D_x b_0=0.

  mKdV: h=1/2 v^4-1/2 v_x^2,
        J_1=-3/2 v^4-v v_xx+1/2 v_x^2, N_2=3h,
        b_0=v v_x;
        N_2+J_1+D_x b_0=0.

The Boussinesq row invokes an external Lagrangian and the scripts; no
self-contained calculation of that row is written in the paper. It
has not been certified by this written-proof audit.

6. Lemma 5.4 fails for "up to total derivatives"
------------------------------------------------

Let

  L(w)=1/2(w_t^2-w_x^2)+D_x f(w), f(w)=1/2 w^2,
  L_d=phi psi_t+f(psi)-f(phi).

This is admissible. The spatial momentum and Hamiltonian are

  pi=-w_x+w,
  H=-1/2(w_t^2+w_x^2).

The sewing equations reduce to

  phi_x=psi_t, psi_x=phi_t,

so h=H_--H_+=0 identically after sewing. The defect satisfies (3.3).
The bulk has the massless dilation symmetry modulo the displayed
spatial divergence: the kinetic term and D_x f have different weights.

Use the active representative of the dilation on histories,
(delta_s Y)(t)=Y(st). The bulk kinetic action in two dimensions has
weight zero, so the common bulk action factor is c(s)=1. Changing
variables in S_d[delta_s Y] gives

  L_d^(s)=phi psi_t+s^{-1}[f(psi)-f(phi)].

Using the inverse convention replaces s^{-1} by s and gives the same
counterexample. With a=1-s^{-1}, the new sewing equations are

  phi_x=psi_t+a phi, psi_x=phi_t+a psi.

The new spatial Hamiltonian difference is

  h_s=a(psi phi_t-phi psi_t)+1/2 a^2(psi^2-phi^2).

It is not a total t derivative when a!=0:

  E_phi h_s=-2a psi_t-a^2 phi,
  E_psi h_s=2a phi_t+a^2 psi.

There are no constraints C to absorb these terms.

Verdict: wrong as written. A bulk spatial divergence changes the
boundary generating function. Scaling just S_d by c(s) cannot account
for an additive boundary counterterm.

Suggested replacement at lines 281--283:

  "Assume strict homogeneity of the chosen bulk Lagrangian
  representatives. If homogeneity is only modulo D_t A+D_x B, include
  the induced boundary contributions B_-(0)-B_+(0) in the transformed
  defect action before asserting the transformed sewing and balance
  identities."

The counterexample concerns the stated general lemma, not the strictly
homogeneous KdV, mKdV or NLS representatives.

For Lemma 5.5 the tensor calculation is correct under its explicit
stress-potential assumptions:

  Lambda epsilon Lambda^T=epsilon
  implies Lambda epsilon=epsilon Lambda^{-T}.

This transports the potential identity. To get Definition 3.1 for
an entire boosted sewing system, the transported potentials must be
local functions of its allowed boundary data, and the identity must
hold for its arbitrary constrained boundary jets. The tensor identity
alone is an identity on the promoted solutions being transformed.

7. Lemma 6.3 does not cover the wave systems it claims to include
----------------------------------------------------------------

For q_t=p, p_t=q_xx-V'(q), the system has spatial order 2, with

  dF/d(q_xx,p_xx) = [[0,0],[1,0]].

This matrix is singular. Thus the printed "this includes" clause is
false and the displayed top-order proof is inapplicable to the
relativistic examples. For a genuinely invertible leading matrix the
usual highest-jet argument is correct (differentiate with respect to
the highest jet if F is nonlinear in it).

The wave conclusion is repairable independently. Let R depend on jets
q_j up to order r and p_j up to order r-1, choosing r large enough.
In

  D_t R=sum_j R_{q_j} p_j
        +sum_j R_{p_j}(q_{j+2}-D_x^j V'(q)),

the independent highest variables p_r and q_{r+1} have coefficients
R_{q_r} and R_{p_{r-1}}. They must both vanish. Induction reduces R
to a constant. This argument works componentwise with the identity
matrix as the leading q_xx coefficient.

There is also a route to the claimed admissible-bulk generality:
admissibility makes E(L)=0 a spatial normal form for D_x^{2K} phi.
Its leading matrix is (-1)^K L_{phi_{0,K} phi_{0,K}}. The terms
involving t derivatives have spatial order at most 2K-1 because there
are no mixed derivatives of spatial order K in L. On this formal
equation manifold the t-jets of

  phi,D_x phi,...,D_x^{2K-1} phi

are free coordinates. Highest t-jet splitting then gives ker D_t=R
for differential functions with no explicit x dependence. This
argument needs to be written and its locality/domain stated.

Verdict: the wave inclusion is wrong, and the application in
Proposition 4.2(A)/Theorem 6.4 is not justified by the printed lemma.
This is a proof gap with an available repair, not a counterexample
to the no-first-integral conclusion for ordinary massive waves.

Suggested replacement at lines 311--313:

  State separate invertible-symbol and wave-system cases, and give
  the weighted-jet argument above for the latter; alternatively
  prove the spatial-normal-form version for admissible Lagrangians.

Theorem 6.4 also needs the residual normalisation from Proposition
4.2 for the relevant constituent laws, not merely an instruction to
subtract a constant from the generating density.

9. Lemma 6.5 and Theorem 8.1(C): missing distinct weights
-------------------------------------------------------

The last inference in Lemma 6.5 requires w!=0. The statement does not
assume that. If w=0, all exponents w_0+n w coincide. Nonzero symbols
at different orders need not be linearly independent. For example,

  m(k;epsilon)=epsilon/(1-epsilon) m_*(k)

has infinitely many nonzero coefficients but all are the same symbol.
Its coefficients are covariant with the same weight. This is a
counterexample to the claimed linear-algebra implication, not an
assertion that this series is realised by a particular published defect.

Theorem 8.1(C) imports that implication: covariance of the family
under an unspecified one-parameter point symmetry does not require
that the parameter moves at all. Time translations can preserve an
autonomous family and supply no independent weights.

The proof also appeals to uniqueness in Lemma 6.2, but an arbitrary
formal branch in Definition 6.1 is not assumed to arise from that
implicit-function system. One must either assume covariance of the
selected branch or prove it (e.g. by uniqueness).

Verdict: the unconditional independence inference is wrong; the
independence part of Theorem 8.1 is not established as stated.

Suggested replacement at lines 323--325 and 408:

  "In the symmetry alternative assume w!=0, covariance of the
  selected formal branch, and covariance of the generating density
  with the stated weight. Then the nonzero quadratic coefficients
  have distinct weights. Otherwise assume directly that infinitely
  many quadratic symbols are linearly independent."

10. Nonresonance and rigidity
-----------------------------

There is a simple all-degree proof of (NR) for the nonaffine scalar
dispersion branches listed. Eliminate the last momentum:

  k_n=-s_n sum_{i<n} s_i k_i.

If Omega vanished on an open part of the momentum hyperplane, then
for distinct i,j<n,

  partial_i partial_j Omega
     =s_n s_i s_j omega_{j_n}''(k_n)=0.

For n>=3 there are two such independent variables. Thus omega_{j_n}
would be affine on an interval. Analyticity contradicts this for
k^2, k^3, sqrt(k^2+m^2) with m>0, and k sqrt(1+k^2).
This proves the all-n condition, rather than inferring it from the
degree-3 and degree-4 script checks.

Lemma 7.2 is correct in the polynomial/formal analytic, translation-
invariant setting described, with continuous mode kernels on the
regular spectral patches. Its independent argument is:

  lowest degree d>=3: {F_d,H_2}=0;
  Fourier polarisation: Omega c=0;
  nonresonance: c=0 on a dense open subset;
  continuity: c=0 everywhere.

The remaining density is a total derivative by the local variational
criterion. For an analytic germ, this argument is applied degree by
degree. It does not establish a result for arbitrary C-infinity
densities flat at the vacuum.

11. Theorem 7.3: a false intermediate assertion with linear terms
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Conservation of Q=Q_1+Q_2+... gives at degree two

  {Q_2,H_2}+{Q_1,H_3}=0,

not {Q_2,H_2}=0. Condition (L) mentions the Q_m, not H.

Explicit local polynomial example:

Take canonical real fields q,p and

  G[p]=integral p p_x^2 dx,
  f[p]=delta G/delta p=-p_x^2-2p p_xx.

The local change of variables

  U=q+f[p], P=p

is canonical: shifting q by a variational gradient of p is a
canonical transformation. Equivalently it is the time-one canonical
flow generated by G. Pull back the free Schrödinger Hamiltonian:

  H=1/2 integral [(q+f[p])_x^2+p_x^2] dx.

This is polynomial, local, and has

  H_2=1/2 integral(q_x^2+p_x^2) dx,
  H_3=integral q_x f_x dx,
  H_4=1/2 integral f_x^2 dx.

Its linearised frequencies are +/-k^2, with nondegenerate normal
modes on a dense set; hence the stated (NR) and (NR2) hold.

The local charge

  Q=integral U dx=integral(q+p_x^2) dx

is conserved, since in the free coordinates U_t=-P_xx. Also

  {Q_1,Q}=integral (-2p_xx) dx=0.

If desired take the two-charge collection Q and the momentum

  R=integral p q_x dx.

R is conserved by translation invariance and {Q_1,R}=integral q_x=0,
while R_1=0. Thus (L) holds for the entire collection.

Nevertheless, direct functional differentiation gives

  {Q_2,H_2}=-2 integral p_xx q_xx dx,
  {Q_1,H_3}=+2 integral p_xx q_xx dx.

The first expression is nonzero, for example for q=p=cos x on the
circle. The two terms cancel exactly as conservation requires.
Therefore the sentence "Each Q_{n,2} is conserved by the linearised
flow" is false even under (NR), (NR2) and (L).

This counterexample invalidates a step of the proof; its charges
Q,R do commute, so it does not refute the theorem's conclusion.

The alternative in which the quadratic parts are assumed directly
to commute does work: (L) removes all brackets involving the linear
parts, so the bracket B starts above degree two, and rigidity applies.

Verdict: unclear for the (NR2)-only alternative; correct under direct
quadratic commutation and the analytic/formal regularity needed by
Lemma 7.2.

Suggested replacement at lines 370--376:

  "Assume the densities are polynomial or analytic/formal at the
  vacuum. In the (NR2) alternative also assume
  {Q_{n,1},H}=0 for every n (for example, that each linear part is a
  Casimir), or directly assume {Q_{n,2},H_2}=0."

With the additional assumption, the degree-two conservation identity
does give linearised conservation of Q_{n,2}, and the printed argument
can proceed.

12. Independence: correct existence argument, wrong universal wording
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The evaluation-matrix proof in Theorem 7.4 is sound for a fixed
finite-mode seed with the required nonzero mode amplitudes: choose
momenta where the symbol-evaluation matrix has full rank and scale
that seed by a nonzero small parameter. A nonzero determinant
persists, and independence holds in a neighbourhood.

It is not true "at all sufficiently small fields supported on

  Q_0=1/2 integral u^2 dx,
  Q_1=1/2 integral u_x^2 dx.

Their symbols 1 and k^2 are independent functions, but at
u=epsilon cos(kx),

  delta Q_1=k^2 delta Q_0.

Such fields are arbitrarily small and supported in any selected
finite Fourier space that contains this mode. At the vacuum both
gradients vanish.

Verdict: wrong universal quantifier; the claimed existence of
functionally independent charges follows from the proof.

Suggested replacement at lines 378--380:

  "There exists a finite-mode seed v_0 such that the gradients are
  independent at epsilon v_0 for every sufficiently small nonzero
  epsilon, and hence in a neighbourhood of each such field."

13. Proposition 7.5: the zero-mode claim needs qualifications
-------------------------------------------------------------

Taylor expansion at a perturbed field v=eta v_1+eta^2 v_2 gives

  Q[v]_{eta^2}=Q_2[v_1]+Q_1[v_2].

Thus a linear density need not average to zero on a second-order
zero mode, and that correction cannot simply be discarded.

For the canonical example in item 11, a linear free wave in U,P
corresponds to q=U-f[P], so

  average(q)=-average(P_x^2),
  average(q+P_x^2)=0.

The quadratic part of Q in the independent coordinates q,p is
integral p_x^2, which is nonzero on the first-order wave. The
second-order correction to q cancels it in the on-solution average.
This demonstrates the danger in identifying an on-solution amplitude
expansion with the quadratic Taylor coefficient.

A pure first-order-wave substitution computes the quadratic symbol
if it is made in independent initial-data variables, after reducing
all temporal derivatives using the full nonlinear evolution.
Alternatively, one can use an actual nonlinear solution and subtract
Q_1[v_2]. The written prescription does not state either convention,
and its assertion about second-order corrections is not generally valid.
No counterexample to a particular published generating-symbol entry
is claimed here.

Verdict: unclear in the stated generality. State the phase-space
representative and the treatment of linear terms, or assume all
linear parts vanish modulo total derivatives.

Suggested replacement at lines 382--384:

  "Reduce the generating density to independent Hamiltonian
  initial-data variables using the full nonlinear field equations.
  Evaluate its quadratic Taylor coefficient on the chosen normal
  mode, holding all higher-order initial-data corrections zero.
  If an on-solution amplitude expansion is used instead, subtract
  the contribution of the linear functional on the second-order
  initial data."

14. The assertion after Theorem 8.1 is too strong
-------------------------------------------------

Line 415 says that (A) and (D) concern the bulk alone and hold for
all massive relativistic scalar theories. But (A) contains either
(NR2) or a commutation condition on the defect-generated charges.
The latter is not a statement about the bulk alone.

Even a pair of free massive scalar fields with the same mass has
noncommuting quadratic conserved charges. Write K=-D_x^2+m^2 and

  H=1/2 integral[p_1^2+p_2^2+q_1 Kq_1+q_2 Kq_2] dx,
  A=integral[p_1 p_2+q_1 Kq_2] dx,
  B=1/2 integral[p_1^2+q_1 Kq_1-p_2^2-q_2 Kq_2] dx.

Direct differentiation gives {A,H}={B,H}=0, while

  {A,B}=2 integral[p_1 Kq_2-p_2 Kq_1] dx,

which is nonzero. This is the familiar equal-frequency obstruction
to (NR2), obtained here directly, without appealing to literature.
The paper itself acknowledges the degeneracy in the affine Toda row.

Verdict: wrong. The remaining general gap cannot be assigned entirely
to (C) without specifying the charge commutation and linear-part
conditions and the scope of the other assumptions.

Suggested replacement at line 415:

  "Massive relativistic scalar theories have Lorentz closure and
  nonaffine massive dispersion. Degenerate mass branches require a
  separate check of quadratic commutation. The charge-dependent
  conditions in (A) and (D), as well as the family condition (C),
  must be verified."

15. Appendix A: the converse loses a constant
---------------------------------------------

The forward geometric interpretation is correct: pulling back the
Liouville form gives -delta S_d on C=0, so its exterior derivative
vanishes. With no auxiliary fields the Hessian graph is Lagrangian.
Pairing X with the restricted symplectic form is the differential
of H_--H_+, which proves the stated tangency equivalence.

But a density with zero t-Euler derivative is D_t B+c in the
autonomous local algebra, not necessarily D_t B. Definition 3.1
does not explicitly quotient by constants.

Counterexample: take

  L_-=1/2(phi_t^2-phi_x^2),
  L_+=1/2(psi_t^2-psi_x^2)-c, c!=0,
  L_d=phi psi_t.

The sewing equations are phi_x=psi_t and psi_x=phi_t. Differentiating
them gives both wave equations, and the spatial Hamiltonian flow is
tangent. However H_--H_+=-c on the sewing correspondence.
This has zero t-Euler derivative but is not a total t derivative
of an autonomous local function of the boundary fields.

Verdict: the converse at line 466 needs a normalisation or allowance
for explicitly time-dependent defect momentum.

Suggested replacement at line 466:

  "The balance holds up to an additive constant. If the two vacuum
  fluxes agree, that constant vanishes and the defect satisfies
  Definition 3.1. Otherwise an explicitly time-dependent term ct
  in the defect momentum is required."

Convention qualification for item 15: if "local P_tilde(Y)" is intended
to permit explicit t dependence, the term ct is already available and
the converse can be repaired simply by stating that convention. The
counterexample is to the autonomous interpretation, not to that larger
algebra of differential functions.

Scope of the conclusions
------------------------

The central variational proof of Theorem 3.3 survives this audit.
Theorem 8.1 is not justified as printed: the (NR2) involution argument,
the distinct-weight independence inference, and the normalisation/
kernel-D_t applications require repair. Several general supporting
claims also admit the explicit counterexamples above.

No numerical table, all-orders compatibility claim imported from an
external paper, or author script has been certified by these scratch
calculations. In particular, finite checked orders are not proofs of
the formal branch or independence at all orders. These calculations
identify proof obligations; they do not allege that sine-Gordon, NLS,
KdV, mKdV, affine Toda, or Boussinesq lack their known hierarchies.
