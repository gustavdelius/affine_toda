1. Integration: second-pass R5 / Lemma 6.5
----------------------------------------

Location: paper.md lines 338--348.
Verdict: the repaired implication and proof are correct. The example
of a supposedly linear boost action needs the correction in T1 below.

Independent computation:

Let a vacuum-normalised functional have homogeneous expansion

  Q(v)=Q_1(v)+Q_2(v)+O(v^3),

and let the symmetry, on independent phase-space data, have expansion

  g_theta(v)=L_theta v+B_theta(v,v)+O(v^3).

Substitution gives, without any covariance assumptions,

  [Q o g_theta]_2
     =Q_2(L_theta v)+Q_1(B_theta(v,v)).

Thus if the full functional has weight chi(theta), and if the second
summand is a trivial functional, then

  Q_2(L_theta v)=chi(theta) Q_2(v)

as functionals. The new condition 4 imposes precisely this requirement.
Because the vacuum is fixed and g is a group action, differentiation
at the vacuum gives L_(theta+eta)=L_theta L_eta. Its action on quadratic
functionals therefore defines the claimed linear representation.
Coefficient comparison in condition 3 gives

  chi_n(theta)=exp((w_0+n w)theta).

For w nonzero and a fixed nonzero real theta, distinct n give distinct
eigenvalues. A finite relation between the corresponding nonzero
symbols can be separated by applying polynomials in this linear map.
Every coefficient in the relation is zero.

Result: the earlier mixing term is now explicitly removed, so the
eigenvector argument is valid under the revised hypotheses.

T1. Lorentz boosts are not generally linear on initial data
----------------------------------------------------------

Location: paper.md line 342.
Verdict: wrong illustrative assertion, not a failure of the repaired
lemma under its explicit condition 4.

The sentence reads:

  "This holds when the action is linear, as for Lorentz boosts and the
   dilations of Section 5.2, or when the Q_n have no linear parts."

Independent countercalculation:

Consider the Lorentz-invariant scalar Lagrangian

  L=(phi_t^2-phi_x^2)/2-V(phi),
  V(phi)=m^2 phi^2/2+gamma phi^3/3+O(phi^4),

with gamma nonzero. It is admissible: L_(phi_x phi_x)=-1.
The vacuum phi=0 is fixed by boosts. The equation is

  phi_tt=phi_xx-V'(phi).

Choose the active boost convention

  phi_theta(x,t)
     =phi(x cosh(theta)+t sinh(theta),
          t cosh(theta)+x sinh(theta)).

Write q(x)=phi(x,0), p(x)=phi_t(x,0). Differentiation at theta=0 gives

  delta q=x p,
  delta p=q_x+x phi_tt(x,0)
         =q_x+x(q_xx-m^2 q)-gamma x q^2+O(q^3).

The quadratic part of the infinitesimal action on (q,p) is

  (0,-gamma x q^2),

which is nonzero. Consequently B_theta is not identically zero for
small theta. The pullback of unrestricted spacetime functions is
linear, but restriction to solutions and identification with initial
data uses the nonlinear field equation.

Suggested replacement for the last sentence of condition 4:

  "This holds when the induced action on phase-space data is linear,
   as for the dilations of Section 5.2 in the variables used here,
   or when the Q_n have no linear parts. For Lorentz boosts it must
   be checked on phase-space data; the absence of linear parts
   suffices for the sine-Gordon application."

Scope: sine-Gordon has V'(phi)=sin(phi), so its infinitesimal boost
has no quadratic correction at the vacuum. More directly, the charges
used in Section 9 have Q_(n,1)=0, which already makes condition 4
automatic. The above correction does not undermine that application.

2. Integration: second-pass R6 / Proposition 7.5
-----------------------------------------------

Location: paper.md lines 417--421.
Verdict: correct under the newly stated locality and Lemma 6.2
hypotheses, in the autonomous formal setting.

Independent computation:

At the constant vacuum, linearise the local branch equation in Delta.
After the vacuum offset has been removed its linear operator has the
form

  A(epsilon)=A_0+sum_(r>=1) epsilon^r A_r,

where A_0 is an invertible constant matrix and each A_r is a
constant-coefficient differential operator. If the original leading
matrix is pointwise algebraic, its evaluation at the constant vacuum
is still a constant matrix. Reduction of time derivatives by the
linearised evolution preserves constant coefficients.

Its formal inverse C=sum_(r>=0) epsilon^r C_r satisfies

  C_0=A_0^(-1),
  C_n=-A_0^(-1) sum_(r=1)^n A_r C_(n-r).

Every C_n is a constant-coefficient differential operator. Matrix
coefficients need not commute with one another; this recurrence does
not assume that. Each C_n nevertheless commutes with D_x. No inverse
power of D_x is introduced.

Write the evolution as F(v)=A v+F_2(v)+O(v^3). If F_2=D_x B_2,
the difference between full and linear time reduction of a linear
local expression has, at degree two, the form of sums of

  D_x^j A^r F_2,
  D_x^j A^r F_2'[v](A v),

and the analogous iterated terms. Since

  F_2'[v](h)=D_x(B_2'[v](h)),

each such term lies in Im D_x. At degree two, the error in the
branch is obtained by applying C to such a source; it remains in
Im D_x coefficientwise in epsilon. The linearisation of the local
generating density again has constant coefficients at the vacuum,
and therefore preserves this property.

Errors in quadratic expressions already containing two first-order
fields contribute only at degree three or above. Thus the entire
quadratic error in the reduced generating density is a total
x-derivative. Its periodic x-average is zero.

Result: the revised proof supplies the missing justification for the
linear-time-flow shortcut. This is a formal-series conclusion and
does not assert convergence of the branch at finite epsilon.

The KdV example in the revised paragraph is also consistent:

  (2+epsilon D_x)Delta=-2 epsilon u+(epsilon/2)Delta^2

has A_0=2 and satisfies Lemma 6.2. Conversely, on a first harmonic,

  average(u^2)=2 eta^2 |a|^2,
  average(p_t)=average(3u^2-u_xx)=6 eta^2 |a|^2,

so the potential example correctly explains why locality in the
specified independent variables is essential.

3. Integration: second-pass R8 / Boussinesq factors
--------------------------------------------------

Locations: paper.md lines 269, 421 and 479.
Verdict: correct.

Independent calculation from the Lagrangian used in the preceding
review:

  L=u_t^2/(2c^2)-u_x^2/2-lambda u_x^3/3-kappa^2 u_xx^2/2.

For the characteristic Q=t,

  pr(v_Q)L=u_t/c^2=D_t(u/c^2),
  Xi(Q)=t u_t/c^2.

The affine Noether density is therefore

  N=u/c^2-t u_t/c^2,

giving a_0=u/c^2 and G=(u-v)/c^2, exactly as now printed.

The Euler equation gives

  u_tt/c^2=u_xx+lambda D_x(u_x^2)-kappa^2 u_xxxx.

With canonical p=u_t/c^2, this means

  u_t=c^2 p,
  p_t=u_xx+lambda D_x(u_x^2)-kappa^2 u_xxxx.

Hence F_2=(0,lambda D_x(u_x^2)), as corrected at line 421.
The symbol table fixes c=1, so neither repair changes its numerical
normalisation.

4. Integration: second-pass R1 and R2
------------------------------------

Locations: paper.md lines 247--249 and 453.
Verdict: the requested qualifications have been made.

At a constant vacuum pair, autonomous Q^1 and Q^2 give

  D_t Q^1=0, D_x Q^2=0.

For the autonomous a_0,b_0 supplied by Definition 5.1, closure gives
N_2=-J_1 at that pair. Consequently

  R_2=D_x Q^2+Delta N_2
     =-Delta J_1
     =-D_t Q^1
     =0.

This is the vacuum-normalisation argument now explicitly restricted
to autonomous charges. It establishes the use made of it by the
affine-current construction.

For the clarification about condition (E), if Q_(n,1)=0, conditions
(L) and (L') vanish identically. For KdV, a linear part proportional
to integral(u dx) is a Casimir, because

  delta integral(u dx)/delta u=1,  J(1)=D_x(1)=0.

Its bracket with every local functional is zero. The revised
paragraph therefore correctly distinguishes the four discussed
applications from the rows where charge conditions remain open.
