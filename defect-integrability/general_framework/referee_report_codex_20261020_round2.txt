2. New spatial-normal-form and residual-normalisation proofs
------------------------------------------------------------

Verdict: correct, with the formal/local and autonomous conventions
already used in these arguments.

Independent derivation of Lemma 6.3:

  E(L)=sum_{a,k}(-D_t)^a(-D_x)^k L_{phi_{a,k}}.

When k<K, every term has spatial order at most K+k<=2K-1.
Terms with a>0 and k=K are absent by admissibility. The remaining
top term is

  (-D_x)^K L_{phi_{0,K}}
  =(-1)^K L_{phi_{0,K} phi_{0,K}} phi_{0,2K}
     + terms of lower spatial order.

The top coefficient is invertible. Solving for phi_{0,2K} defines
a formal evolution in x for the 2K variables

  phi,phi_x,...,D_x^{2K-1}phi.

The right hand side may involve their t derivatives. Repeated
differentiation determines all higher x jets, without a relation
among the t jets of these 2K variables. There is only one x-evolution
system to prolong, so no additional compatibility equation is
introduced by the reduction.

For a local R, choose the largest t-order A among those free jets
appearing in its reduced expression. In D_t R, the free jets with
t-order A+1 occur with coefficients R_{phi_{A,j}}. These vanish.
Descending induction eliminates all jet dependence. On a connected
domain, and with no explicit x,t, R is constant.

Independent check of the new end of Proposition 4.3:

  R_2=D_x Q^2+Delta N_2.

At the constant branch vacuum, autonomous differential functions
have D_t and D_x equal to zero. Therefore

  R_2(vac)=Delta N_2(vac)
          =-Delta J_1(vac)       by closure
          =-D_t Q^1(vac)=0      by the defect-charge identity.

This is the required residual normalisation, rather than a claim
that subtracting a constant from G somehow changes R_2.

Definition 6.1 now also requires E[psi]=0. Consequently Theorem
6.4 no longer silently invokes (I) for every family member: both
bulk equations are part of the branch hypothesis. Proposition 4.2's
calculation itself only needs those equations. The coefficientwise
conservation argument then goes through.

If explicitly time-dependent defect charges are to be allowed here,
the "automatic" normalisation needs an extra hypothesis. The current
argument is correct for Q_d(Y) as an autonomous function of boundary
jets, which is the interpretation used above.

3. Trivial currents, KdV mass, and shift symmetries
--------------------------------------------------

Verdict: the revised distinction and the KdV decomposition are correct.

For L=1/2 p_x p_t-p_x^3-1/2 p_xx^2,

  pi_0=1/2 p_t-3p_x^2+p_xxx,
  pi_1=-p_xx.

The constant-shift Noether current is

  (N_s,J_s)=(-1/2 p_x,-pi_0).

Thus

  -(N_s,J_s)+1/2(D_x p,-D_t p)
    =(p_x,pi_0-1/2 p_t)
    =(p_x,p_xxx-3p_x^2),

which is precisely the mass law. For the gyroscopic term
(q p_t-p q_t)/4, a common constant shift gives

  A_d=(p-q)/4,
  Xi_d(1)=(q-p)/4,
  Q_{d,shift}=(p-q)/2.

The remaining defect terms depend on p-q and derivatives, and
therefore have zero variation under this constant shift. Combining
the two laws gives Q_{d,mass}=q-p.

For a common time-linear shift, the variation of a difference-only
term vanishes, but the variation of the first t derivatives is 1.
The extra partial-derivative condition stated at line 218 is indeed
needed. Higher t derivatives have zero variation. Nothing in this
check relies on the new check_scope.py script.

4. The repaired involution and independence arguments
-----------------------------------------------------

Verdict: correct in the stated polynomial/analytic formal setting.

With constant J, {F_a,G_b} has degree a+b-2. For a conserved Q,

  0=[{Q,H}]_2={Q_2,H_2}+{Q_1,H_3}.

The new (L') kills exactly the second term. It is weaker than
{Q_1,H}=0, but no stronger condition is needed at this step.
Under (NR2), Q_2 is then an action functional.

For B={Q_n,Q_m}, (L), degree by degree, removes every contribution
involving the linear parts. Thus B_0=B_1=0 and

  B_2={Q_{n,2},Q_{m,2}}=0,

either by the explicit alternative or by the action argument.
Jacobi gives {B,H}=0. Rigidity makes B vanish as a local functional.

For independence, select a finite set of nonzero momenta where the
symbol-evaluation matrix has a nonzero maximal minor, and a seed
with nonzero amplitudes at those momenta. The corresponding
gradient minor has a nonzero leading coefficient in the small
amplitude. It remains nonzero for sufficiently small nonzero
amplitude and in a neighbourhood. This proves the revised
existence statement of Theorem 7.4.

Theorem 8.1 now explicitly assumes infinitely many independent
nonzero symbols in (C) and the necessary charge assumptions in (E).
With (C) established directly, the original main-theorem objections
are resolved. An application that uses Lemma 6.5 to establish (C)
still needs the repair below.

5. Remaining issue: nonlinear symmetries mix linear and quadratic parts
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Location: paper.md lines 343--345, Lemma 6.5.
Verdict: unclear in its stated generality; its inference about quadratic
symbols is not justified merely by the symmetry fixing the vacuum.

Let the induced transformation of initial data be

  g_theta(v)=L_theta v+B_theta(v,v)+O(v^3).

For Q=Q_1+Q_2+..., its quadratic Taylor coefficient after pullback is

  [Q o g_theta]_2
    =Q_2[L_theta v]+Q_1[B_theta(v,v)].

Therefore the full-charge identity g_theta^*Q=e^{lambda theta}Q
implies

  Q_2[L_theta v]
    =e^{lambda theta}Q_2[v]-Q_1[B_theta(v,v)].

The last term is absent from the proof. Fixing the vacuum only
eliminates the constant term of g_theta, not B_theta.

A concrete example using a symmetry of an admissible Lagrangian
shows that this is a real issue in the asserted intermediate step.
Start with two massless fields

  L=U_t V_t-U_x V_x.

Make the local point change U=q+r^2, V=r. Then

  L=(q_t+2r r_t)r_t-(q_x+2r r_x)r_x,

whose spatial Hessian is [[0,-1],[-1,-4r]], of determinant -1.
The exact symmetry U->a U, V->a^{-1} V fixes the vacuum. In q,r it is

  q'=a q+(a-a^{-2})r^2,
  r'=a^{-1}r.

The canonical momenta are

  P_q=r_t, P_r=q_t+4r r_t.

The local conserved charge

  Q=integral U_t dx
   =integral(P_r-2r P_q) dx

transforms exactly as Q->a Q. Its quadratic part is

  Q_2=-2 integral r P_q dx.

The linearised symmetry has r->a^{-1}r and P_q->a^{-1}P_q, so

  Q_2[L v]=a^{-2} Q_2[v],

not a Q_2[v]. The missing term comes from the quadratic term in P_r':

  P_r'=a P_r-2r(a-a^{-2})P_q.

This example disproves the intermediate inference about a nonlinear
vacuum-fixing symmetry and a conserved charge. It is not presented
as a counterexample satisfying every defect-family assumption of
Lemma 6.5 or as a counterexample to Theorem 8.1.

Suggested replacement/addition at lines 338--345:

  "Assume in addition that the induced action on initial data is
  linear to second order, or that every Q_{n,1} vanishes, or more
  generally that Q_{n,1}[B_theta(v,v)] is a trivial functional.
  Then the covariance of the full charges implies covariance of
  their quadratic symbols under the linearised action."

One can instead assume the desired covariance of quadratic symbols
directly. The integrated closed-one-form argument for the full
charges is not the part being challenged.

This does not obstruct the four principal examples: sine-Gordon,
NLS and mKdV have no linear charge parts, and the KdV dilation is
linear on u. Moreover their displayed symbols establish independence
directly, without Lemma 6.5.

6. Proposition 7.5: valid local calculation, but specify the shortcut
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Location: paper.md lines 416--420.

The original Q_1[v_2] problem is resolved by the fixed-initial-data
prescription. If one first computes the local reduced phase-space
density and then takes its quadratic Taylor coefficient at eta v_1,
the proposed averaging operation is correct.

The new sufficient condition on F_2 is also correct for unreduced
expressions that are local differential expressions in the same
independent variables v. Write

  D_t=delta_1+delta_2+...,
  delta_j v=F_j[v].

At degree two, replacing D_t by delta_1 changes only linear pieces
of an expression. Each error term is built from constant-coefficient
spatial differential operators, delta_1, and one occurrence of
delta_2. If F_2=D_x B_2, delta_2 commutes with D_x, and delta_1
also preserves Im D_x. Every such error is therefore in Im D_x.
The zero Fourier coefficient is unchanged.

There are two distinctions the printed prescription should make:

  (i) Reducing a known local expression and solving an implicit
      branch equation are not the same operation. To transport
      the argument through branch solving, show that the relevant
      linear inverse preserves Im D_x. A regular formal implicit
      problem with invertible algebraic leading coefficient, as
      in Lemma 6.2, supplies this. Definition 6.1 alone does not
      supply such an inverse or uniqueness of the zero mode.

  (ii) A potential is not in general a local differential expression
       in the Hamiltonian variable used to state F_2.

For example, for u=p_x in KdV, the gauge-fixed potential equation is

  p_t=3u^2-u_xx,

whereas u_t=6u u_x-u_xxx has F_2=3D_x(u^2). On a nonzero first
harmonic u=eta(a E+bar(a)E^{-1}),

  average(p_t)=6 eta^2 |a|^2,

but replacing p_t by its linearised value -u_xx gives zero.
The operation D_x^{-1} has removed the spatial derivative from F_2.
Thus "F_2 is a total derivative" does not justify linearising time
derivatives of an unreduced potential. This example tests the
claimed shortcut, not a particular defect generating density.

For source verification, equation (9.4) of Corrigan--Zambon reads
exactly:

  p_t-3p_x^2+p_xxx=0.

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/nlin/0512038, equation (9.4).
The displayed mean is my direct substitution into that equation.

Verdict: correct after full local phase-space reduction; unclear as
a blanket instruction to linearise time derivatives while solving
the original potential-field sewing system. I am not alleging that
the KdV table entry is wrong; item 7 derives it without this shortcut.

Suggested replacement at lines 416--418:

  "First reduce the branch and generating density to local functions
  of the independent Hamiltonian variables using the full evolution.
  The quadratic symbol is their quadratic Taylor coefficient on a
  single normal mode. The shortcut replacing D_t by its linearised
  flow is valid when all expressions and formal branch-solving
  inverses involved preserve Im D_x; in particular, no inverse
  spatial derivative from a potential is used without separate
  justification."

7. Direct checks of three generating symbols and their independence
--------------------------------------------------------------------

These derivations use the written sine-Gordon defect and two spatial
relations from the original primary source. No author script is used.

Sine-Gordon:

Put epsilon=sigma, z=k+omega=e^vartheta, omega^2-k^2=1.
Adding the linearised sewing equations from the defect at line 302
gives

  i(k+omega)(phi+psi)=-(phi-psi)/epsilon.

Thus the first-harmonic multiplier is

  r=psi/phi=(1+i epsilon z)/(1-i epsilon z).

The quadratic part of the defect energy is

  (phi-psi)^2/(4 epsilon)+epsilon(phi+psi)^2/4.

For a real wave eta(a E+bar(a)E^{-1}), its average coefficient is

  |1-r|^2/(2 epsilon)+epsilon |1+r|^2/2
   =2 epsilon(z^2+1)/(1+epsilon^2 z^2)
   =4 epsilon omega z/(1+epsilon^2 z^2).

This is the printed symbol. Its odd coefficients are a common
nonzero factor omega times distinct powers of z.

KdV:

The source equation (9.12) is exactly

  p_x+q_x=2 alpha+1/2(p-q)^2.

Set u=p_x, alpha=-epsilon^{-2}, q=p-2/epsilon+Delta. It becomes

  (2+epsilon D_x)Delta=-2epsilon u+(epsilon/2)Delta^2.

The first-harmonic multipliers are

  d_+=-2epsilon/(2+i epsilon k),
  d_-=-2epsilon/(2-i epsilon k).

The quadratic zero mode is

  (epsilon/4) 2d_+d_- |a|^2
    =2epsilon^3 |a|^2/(4+epsilon^2 k^2).

Since G=q-p, after subtracting the vacuum constant this is exactly
the printed m(k;epsilon). No time-evolution substitution is needed.

mKdV:

The spatial part of source equation (7.16) is exactly

  p_x+q_x=alpha sin(p-q).

With alpha=epsilon^{-1}, u=p_x and Delta=q-p,

  (1+epsilon D_x)Delta=-2epsilon u+O(Delta^3).

Thus d_+d_-=4epsilon^2/(1+epsilon^2 k^2). The momentum generator
is epsilon^{-1}(cos Delta-1), whose quadratic average is

  -epsilon^{-1}d_+d_- |a|^2
    =-4epsilon |a|^2/(1+epsilon^2 k^2).

Source for both spatial relations:
https://arxiv.org/html/nlin/0512038, equations (9.12) and (7.16).
The expansions above are independent calculations from those
relations, not quotations of a generating-function result.

Verdict: these three printed symbols are correct, with the displayed
normalisations. Their nonzero coefficients have independent powers
of k^2 or e^vartheta, proving independence directly. The same
elementary independence test applies to the printed NLS expression;
I do not certify its normalisation from the ambiguous signs in the
source's displayed number-charge formula in this pass.

8. Boussinesq: closure can be derived in writing; two scale factors
------------------------------------------------------------------

Location: paper.md lines 269 and 420.
Verdict: the closure mechanism is correct; the two factors below
need correction or an explicit change of normalisation.

The primary source, equation (2.1), reads

  L= u_t^2/(2c^2)-u_x^2/2-lambda u_x^3/3-kappa^2 u_xx^2/2.

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03772, equation (2.1).

Direct variation gives

  pi_0=-u_x-lambda u_x^2+kappa^2 u_xxx,
  pi_1=-kappa^2 u_xx.

For the time-linear shift Q=t, pr(v_Q)L=u_t/c^2=D_t(u/c^2).
Its Noether current, in the paper's convention, is

  N=u/c^2-t u_t/c^2,
  J=-t pi_0.

Consequently

  a_1=b_1=0, a_2=-u_t/c^2, b_2=-pi_0,
  a_0=u/c^2, b_0=0.

Law 2 is precisely the constant-shift Noether law. Closure follows
from a_2+D_t a_0=0, independently of any script. For law 1=0 choose
Q^1=0, and Proposition 4.3 gives

  G=(u-v)/c^2.

The table instead prints a_0=u/c^2 and G=u-v in the same row.
These are inconsistent unless c=1, or the generating current has
been rescaled by c^2 and that rescaling is stated.

Next, the phase variables in Section 7.1 are canonical (u,p), with
p=u_t/c^2. Their equations are

  u_t=c^2 p,
  p_t=u_xx+lambda D_x(u_x^2)-kappa^2 u_xxxx.

Thus in those variables

  F_2=(0,lambda D_x(u_x^2)),

not (0,c^2 lambda D_x(u_x^2)) as printed at line 420. The latter
formula is for the variables (u,u_t).

Suggested replacements:

  line 269: print G=(u-v)/c^2, or explicitly rescale the entire
            affine current by c^2 and change a_0 accordingly;

  line 420: print F_2=(0,lambda D_x(u_x^2)) in the canonical
            variables used in Section 7.1.

Neither issue affects the property F_2 in Im D_x. The table of
quadratic symbols already sets c=1, so these corrections do not
change that specialised table.

Assessment
----------

The substantive repairs to Theorem 3.3's scope, the formal branch,
the hierarchy normalisation, the no-first-integral lemma, and the
involution theorem are accepted. The revised main theorem is a sound
conditional assembly when its independent-symbol hypothesis is
verified directly in the local/autonomous formal setting.

I would not yet endorse the paper's entire collection of general
proofs without the nonlinear-symmetry qualification in Lemma 6.5
and clarification of the branch-solving shortcut in Proposition 7.5.
Neither remaining issue is a counterexample to the known hierarchies.
The Boussinesq discrepancies are normalisation errors, not failures
of closure or of the total-derivative argument.
