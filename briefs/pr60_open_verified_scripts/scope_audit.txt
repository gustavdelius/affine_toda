## The defect matrix as a gauge transformation
**[Theorem for type I$_-$; Numerical for type I$_+$]** Let $\vec\phi$ and $\vec\psi$ satisfy the defect conditions (-@eq-defect-conditions) of either type on an open region, and let $\mathcal A_\pm$ be the Lax pair (-@eq-lax-pair) with $\beta\to b$. Then, for all $\lambda$, the defect matrix (-@eq-defect-lax-matrix) satisfies
$$
\partial_\pm L_d=L_d\,\mathcal A_\pm(\vec\psi)-\mathcal A_\pm(\vec\phi)\,L_d .
$$ {#eq-dc-gauge}
For type I$_-$ the $\partial_+$ equation is equivalent to the first half of the Bäcklund transformation in components above, and the $\partial_-$ equation to the second half. Wherever $L_d$ is invertible, which fails only at finitely many $\lambda$, the curvature $[\partial_++\mathcal A_+,\partial_-+\mathcal A_-]$ of $\vec\psi$ is $L_d^{-1}$ times that of $\vec\phi$ times $L_d$. So $\vec\psi$ solves the field equation exactly when $\vec\phi$ does.

**[Theorem]** For both types of massive $a_r^{(1)}$ defect at imaginary coupling, with $\beta\ne0$ and $\sigma\in\mathbb C^\times$,

## Absorption and emission
**[Theorem]** Consider a type-I$_\mp$ defect of class $p$ with real $\theta_d$, a soliton of species $a$, and write $\theta_{\rm abs}=\theta_d+i\chi$.

1. At $\theta=\theta_{\rm abs}$ two configurations solve the defect conditions for all $t$, as meromorphic solutions, wherever their $\tau$-functions are nonzero: the soliton on $x<0$ with $\vec\psi$ constant, and $\vec\phi$ constant with the soliton on $x>0$. If $a+2p\not\equiv0$ mod $h$, then $\cos\chi\ne0$, and in both configurations $|E|$ moves with the velocity $\tanh\theta_d$. For $\theta_d>0$ they describe absorption from the left and emission to the right; for $\theta_d<0$, emission to the left and absorption from the right; for $\theta_d=0$ the envelope is stationary. Absorbed and emitted solitons move in the direction of $\operatorname{sign}\theta_d$, for every species and class. If $a+2p\equiv0$ mod $h$, then $\cos\chi=0$ and $|E|$ is constant: the two configurations solve the conditions but have no localized envelope, and describe neither absorption nor emission.
2. $\operatorname{Re}\cosh\theta_{\rm abs}=\cosh\theta_d\,\sin\frac{\pi(a+2p)}h$. If this is positive, the object is the species-$a$ soliton at $\theta_{\rm abs}$, absorption changes the class from $p$ to $p+a$, and the defect stores $M_a(\cosh\theta_{\rm abs},\sinh\theta_{\rm abs})$. If it is negative, the object is the soliton of species $\bar a$ at $\theta_{\rm abs}+i\pi$, absorbed by the class $p+a$; a soliton of species $a$ is then never absorbed by a defect of class $p$.
3. $\theta_{\rm abs}$ is real, and then equal to $\theta_d$, exactly when $a+2p\equiv h/2$ mod $2h$. This requires $h$ even and $a\equiv h/2$ mod 2, and then exactly one class absorbs species $a$ at real rapidity: the species $h/2-2q$ is absorbed by the class $q$ mod $h$. Without a charged jump only the self-conjugate species is absorbed at real rapidity, but, for example, species 1 of $a_5^{(1)}$ is absorbed at $\theta=\theta_d$ by a defect of class 1.
:::

CANDIDATE prp-dpt-vacuum part8-defects/44-particle-transmission.qmd 138
::: {#prp-dpt-vacuum}
## The vacuum of the real-coupling defect
**[Theorem]** At real coupling and for real fields, $\mathcal B_d^{\rm I_\mp}(\vec\phi,\vec\psi;\sigma)\ge0$ for $\sigma>0$, with equality only at $\vec\phi=\vec\psi=0$. So $\vec\phi=\vec\psi=0$ is the unique minimum of the static energy. For $\sigma<0$, $\mathcal B_d$ is unbounded below.
:::

::: {.proof}
In $\mathcal B_d=\frac m{\beta^2}\sum_j[\sigma(f_j-1)+\sigma^{-1}(g_j-1)]$ the exponents of $f_j$ and of $g_j$ sum to zero over $j$, since $\sum_j\vec\alpha_j=0$. By convexity, $\sum_j(f_j-1)\ge\sum_j\log f_j=0$, with equality only if every $f_j=1$, and likewise for the $g_j$. All $g_j=1$ means $\mathsf D(\vec\phi-\vec\psi)=0$, so $\vec\phi=\vec\psi$ because $\mathsf D$ is invertible. Then all $f_j=1$ means $(\mathsf D+\mathsf D^{\mathsf T})\vec\phi=2\vec\phi=0$. Type I$_+$ is $\sigma\to1/\sigma$. The bulk energy is non-negative and vanishes only on constant zero fields. For $\sigma<0$ take $\vec\phi=\vec\psi=t\vec v$ with $\vec v\ne0$. Then every $g_j=1$, while $\sum_j(f_j-1)=\sum_je^{t\beta\vec\alpha_j\cdot\vec v}-h\to\infty$ as $t\to\infty$, because some $\vec\alpha_j\cdot\vec v>0$; so $\mathcal B_d\to-\infty$, for both types. Linear layers of width $e^{-At/2}$, $A=\max_j\beta\vec\alpha_j\cdot\vec v$, join these values to zero at a bulk cost $O(t^2e^{At/2})$, so the total static energy is unbounded below as well. Check (g) samples random fields for $r=2,3$ and both types (`code/defect_particle_transmission.py`).
:::
