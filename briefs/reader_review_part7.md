## B. Explanation gaps and ideas for improvement

**Ch.28.**
- B1. §"boundary state" l.98: "$\tau=ix$ is the new time and $y=-it$ the new space" mixes real and Euclidean coordinates. Write: Euclidean time $\tau_E=-x\ge0$, Euclidean space $y=t_E$; then $|B\rangle$ is the state at $\tau_E=0$. A one-panel figure (half-plane → rotated strip with $|B\rangle$ at the bottom) would carry the whole section.
- B2. l.58–66: no picture of the two orderings that give the reflection equation. Part I has `ch2_f4_factorization`; a twin figure "the two ways of reflecting two particles" (GZ Fig. 10) is the single most useful missing figure in the Part.
- B3. l.156: "the left-hand side of (reflection-braid) without its last scattering" is cryptic. Ch.33 says it better (`M(y_1,y_2)=K\,\check R\,K`). Copy that wording; note the object is $K_2(\theta_1)\check S(\theta_1+\theta_2)K_2(\theta_2)$.
- B4. Add one sentence on why the blocks have "$h=6$" for Lee–Yang: $h$ is just the denominator in `eq-blocks` (period $2h$), not the Coxeter number of anything here.
- B5. Mattsson–Dorey lemmas (l.172): $u$ for the pole position clashes with the fusing angle $u_{ab}^c$ and with $v$ used two paragraphs earlier. "Decay angles" is undefined.
- B6. l.188: $Z\sim g_\alpha g_\beta e^{-LE_0(R)}$ uses both $L$ and $R$; say which is the cylinder length and which the circumference.

**Ch.29.**
- B7. `eq-bsg-k-matrix` (l.59) introduces $\hat\epsilon_j$ and the coideal $b_j$ two chapters before they are explained. Add a boxed preview ("$\hat\epsilon_{0,1}$ are the eigenvalues of the boundary charges on the ground state; ch.32").
- B8. $q$, $\lambda$, $\omega$: this chapter says $\lambda$, ch.32–35 say $\omega$ for the same $\frac{8\pi}{\beta_{\rm SG}^2}-1=\frac{4\pi}{\beta^2}-1$. Add "(the rank-one case of the $\omega$ of Part VI)".
- B9. Dirichlet limit (l.104): the argument that $P^+$ must have a pole at $i\pi/2$ at $\phi_0=\pi/\beta$ deserves two lines, since it is the only physical input behind `eq-bsg-dirichlet`.

**Ch.30.**
- B10. l.11 "every conserved charge of odd parity" is vague (odd under what?). Ch.28 already says: $Q_s-Q_{-s}$.
- B11. l.53: for $d_4^{(1)}$ with $C_j=\pm1$ **no** constant vacuum exists at either coupling. State the consequence ("the ground state is a non-constant static solution; see ch.31") or readers will wonder what the vacuum is.
- B12. l.146: "does not see the root normalization … same as literature's normalizations of twisted roots" is too terse to follow.
- B13. In `tbl-durham` the $c_2^{(1)}=b_2^{(1)}$ row uses $c$-labels; add "(node 1 of $c_2$ = node $n$ of $b_2$)" so the identification of the two branches is checkable.
- B14. Sklyanin's proof is fine but the reader never sees why $K_-(1/\lambda)$ appears. One sentence: the second factor is the transport backwards after reflection.

**Ch.31.**
- B15. l.59–65: the formula for $d$ (`eq-virtual-collision`) follows from the shifts only after correcting the mirror's **pre-collision** phase by $\ln A$ (the usual two-soliton displacement): $d=-(\xi_1+\xi_2+\ln A)/(2m_a\cosh\theta)$, whereas $\Delta t=-(\xi_1+\xi_2)/(m_a\sinh\theta)$ uses the post-collision value. I verified both agree with the printed formulas, but the text says only "fitted numerically". Give this two-line derivation; it also explains the gap between the blue and red lines in the $C=-1$ and Neumann panels of the figure.
- B16. **Attractive/repulsive.** Ch.31 calls $C=+1$ "attractive" (delay, collision behind the wall, boundary breathers) while the classical particle reflection factor has bound-state poles for $C=-1$ and none for $C=+1$ (l.132, l.224), and ch.33 `exr-semiclassical` says that in the usual scattering sense a delay means repulsion. This tension is only visible in an exercise. Move a short paragraph to ch.31 l.65 saying which property each word describes, and perhaps use "soliton-attractive"/"particle-attractive".
- B17. l.75: "the pattern relative to the boundary vacuum is preserved" needs one more line (what is compared to what).
- B18. A table of the boundary types encountered (Neumann, $C=\pm1$, solitonic, soliton-preserving) with vacuum, soliton return, breather and particle poles would pay for itself here; the information is spread over five chapters.

**Ch.32.**
- B19. §"Boundary conformal perturbation theory" (l.19–25): $\tilde\phi=\phi(x)+\phi(-x)$ and "the right perturbation is $V(\phi)$, not $V(\tilde\phi)$" are stated without saying what $\phi$ is on $x\le0$ or why. A reader cannot tell what is being perturbed. Needs a short paragraph or a pointer to a derivation.
- B20. Symbol $K$ is the Cartan generator matrix (l.140: "$k_1=K=\mathrm{diag}(q,q^{-1})$") and the reflection matrix in the very next equations. Rename the generator matrix (e.g. $D$).
- B21. `eq-an-eps-condition` (l.237) contains $\cos(\pi\omega/2)$ with $\omega$ undefined in this chapter (see C).
- B22. **Classical ↔ quantum dictionary is incomplete (l.253–267).** The text says both spaces are discrete with a product as the invariant. Counting classes: classical $\{0\}$, $\prod C=+1$, $\prod C=-1$ (the last coincides with $+1$ for $h$ even); quantum $\{\hat\epsilon=0\}$, $s=+1$, $s=-1$. The conjecture assigns Neumann→$s=+1$ and uniform→$\hat\epsilon=0$, so neither "$s\leftrightarrow\prod C$" nor a bijection holds. State clearly which classical boundary each of the three quantum points is paired with, and that $s=-1$ and the solitonic/$\prod C=-1$ boundaries are unmatched (ch.35 problem 1, 5 are the right places for the open part).
- B23. `prp-dm-first-order` is labelled **[Conjecture]** but followed by a `.proof` environment containing a plausibility argument. Use "Argument" instead of "Proof".

**Ch.33.**
- B24. Block notation changes inside the chapter: $(x)$ of `eq-blocks` (angle $\theta$) vs $(a)_I$ in $\mu$ (l.82). Ch.35 l.24 gives the dictionary $(a)_I=(2a/\omega)$; move it here.
- B25. l.105: the statement that $(-\frac h4\omega)_I$ and $-(\frac h4\omega)_I$ differ by $-(1-i\sinh\theta)/(1+i\sinh\theta)$ — I get the inverse ratio depending on the order; just say "differ by a unitary, crossing-symmetric factor".
- B26. "Which boundary" (l.137) repeats ch.32 l.255–266 almost verbatim. Keep one.
- B27. The $d_n$ discussion (l.160) is hard to follow: the generator-rescaling story fills a paragraph to explain a shift $\eta^2=q^{2-2n}$. Put the net statement first ("their $K$ differs from ours by a spectral-parameter shift") and the derivation after.

**Ch.34.**
- B28. This chapter needs the most scaffolding. A physicist reading cold meets Satake diagrams, admissibility, intersection property, bar involution, quasi-K-matrix, cylindrical bialgebra and "modified nilpotent subalgebra" with one line of definition each. Ideas: (i) an opening box "what you need from this chapter" (four statements); (ii) worked $sl_2$ example of $\mathfrak k=so_2$ / Onsager; (iii) move the heavier citations (Appel–Vlaar cylindrical, Kolb–Yakimov, RV Table 4) into Notes; (iv) say plainly that the section on trigonometric K-matrices is a guide to the literature, not needed for ch.35.
- B29. `prp-serre-obstruction` proof (l.115–128): consider presenting only the three-line weight-$\pm\alpha_j$ and weight-0 summaries and leaving the term-by-term bookkeeping to an exercise; the result and the five consequences are what physics readers need.
- B30. The Onsager limit paragraph (l.189): state that $q=1$ means odd $\lambda$ right at the start, otherwise "classical limit" and "Onsager limit" look like the same thing.

**Ch.35.**
- B31. Display `eq-breather-continuation` writes bare numbers where blocks are meant ("$(h-\frac B2)$", "$(-1+\frac B2)$"), and then equates them to a quotient. Readers will read it as ordinary arithmetic. Use one consistent notation for "block" (the book already writes $(x)$; say so once more here and give the chain $(−n)=(h−1)^{-1}$).
- B32. The a_2 boundary spectrum is the most interesting result of the Part and the most compressed. The table at $B=9/20$ and Fig. 30 are excellent; add one explicit worked step (e.g. $b_{0,0}\to b_{1,0}$: pole, energy, new factor) before `eq-a2-excited`.
- B33. The rotor interpretation (l.230) is the book's own; the text flags it ("this interpretation is ours"). Good. Consider moving the paragraph to the Notes so the main line stays with Delius–Gandenberger.
- B34. Open-problem list: items 1 and 6 (vacua for $\prod C=-1$ and for missing patterns), 3 and 4 (Neumann derivation, duality) are interlinked; a short "priority" sentence for each of the four themes would help a student pick one.

**Part-level ideas.**
- B35. Two diagrams for the whole Part: (a) a "road map" of how soliton reflection matrix → breather → particle amplitude and how classical boundary ↔ coideal ↔ Satake data connect; (b) the tbl of boundary types in B18.
- B36. Add a one-page "Part VII symbol table" (see C) at the start, as Part VI does for $\omega$.
- B37. Several definitions arrive only after use: ε̂ (ch.29), $\omega$ (ch.32), $K$ as coideal rep (ch.32). Forward-pointer boxes are cheaper than reordering.
