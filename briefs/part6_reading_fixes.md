## A. Errors and likely errors

**A1. RSOS positivity criterion (`27-rsos.qmd`, Proposition `prp-rsos-kinks`, part 1 of the statement, and step 1 of its proof; `exr-quantum-dims`).** The proposition says that if $p'\equiv\pm1\pmod p$ then "every $d_h$ is positive". The exercise says all quantum dimensions are positive iff $r\equiv\pm1\pmod{2p}$, where $r=p'-p$. I checked this with `qdim.py` in the scratchpad: for all $4\le p\le30$ and all $r$ coprime to $p$, "all $[k]_q>0$ for $1\le k\le p-1$" holds exactly when $r\equiv\pm1\pmod{2p}$, with no exceptions. The proposition's mod-$p$ criterion fails in 54 cases with $p\le9$; for example $(p,r)=(5,4)$ has $r\equiv-1\pmod5$, but $[2]_q=\sin(8\pi/5)/\sin(4\pi/5)<0$. So part 1 of the proposition, the sign statement "for $p'\equiv-1\pmod p$ all $d_h$ are positive" in the remarks after the proposition, and the criterion in the proof's step 1 must all be restated with $2p$ in place of $p$. Then recheck which couplings the numerics call "$p'\equiv\pm1$" (the eight couplings with Hermitian analyticity to $10^{-12}$, and the five that fail), since they may have used the mod-$p$ test. Also check the example $W_3(7,9)$ ($r=2$, not $\pm1$ mod 14) against the corrected criterion.

**A2. Coupling map in the $e_6^{(2)}$ chapter (`25-e6-solitons.qmd`, Section `sec-e6-result`, the final sentence).** It says the tree term "fixes the coupling map $1/\omega=\beta^2/2\pi$, which is the gradation assumed". The gradation of the same chapter is $\omega=4\pi/\beta^2-1$, which gives $1/\omega=\beta^2/4\pi+O(\beta^4)$. Chapter 26 (`26-f4-solitons.qmd`, Section `sec-one-loop-data-fix`, item 2, and the sentence "the tree term also gives $1/\omega=\beta^2/8\pi$ ... for $c_3^{(1)}$ and $e_6^{(2)}$ the two agree") and the one-loop check of chapter 19 ($\delta H=+3/2\pi$ for $e_6^{(2)}$, $H=18+6/\omega$) all use $\beta^2/4\pi$. Correct chapter 25 to $1/\omega=\beta^2/4\pi$, and state which normalization of $\beta$ is in use.

**A3. "Integer lifts" are half-integers (`26-f4-solitons.qmd`, Section `sec-one-loop-data-fix`, item 2, and Section `sec-soliton-masses-one-loop`).** The text says "with integer lifts there are exactly two solutions", with $(c,c')=(1,\tfrac52)$ for candidate (a) and $(0,-\tfrac12)$ for (b). The zeros of $c$ are at $3\omega+\tfrac52$ and $3\omega-\tfrac12$, so $c'$ is a half-integer. The one-loop solutions $T=6\omega+\tfrac32+6j$ have zeros at $3\omega+\tfrac12+3j$, also half-integer. The lift convention in chapter 21 (`sec-integer-lifts`) and in chapters 23–24 is "shifts by integers from a base point", and the base points here are half-integers. Either define "lift" modulo the lattice $\tfrac12\mathbb Z$ in the same way as the other families, or give the count of solutions with the actual convention. Then recheck the claim "exactly two solutions".

**A4. Who constructed the $\mathbf{27}$ R-matrix (`22-known-smatrices.qmd`, Section `sec-imaginary-coupling-quantum-theory`, table row $f_4^{(1)}$; `26-f4-solitons.qmd`, Section `sec-sm-f4-representation-r-matrix`; `08-r-matrices.qmd`, first paragraph).** Chapter 22 says "the $\mathbf{27}$ R-matrix is known [@gmw1996]". Chapter 26 says Gandenberger, MacKay and Watts "constructed this R-matrix earlier", and "our construction is independent". Chapter 8 lists the $\mathbf{27}$ of $U_q(e_6^{(2)})$ among the representations for which the methods were new and which Part VI handles directly. Check what GMW (1996) contains. If they have it, say so in chapter 8 and remove "new" in chapter 26; if not, correct chapter 22.

**A5. Normalization of the tensor-product-graph exponents (`25-e6-solitons.qmd`, Section `sec-sm-e6-representation-r-matrix`, table of eigenvalues).** I computed the $F_4$ quadratic Casimirs with long roots of squared length 2 (script `f4cas.py` in the scratchpad): $C(\mathbf1)=0$, $C(\mathbf{26})=12$, $C(\mathbf{52})=18=2h^\vee$, $C(\mathbf{273})=24$, $C(\mathbf{324})=26$. The table's eigenvalues are consistent with the graph rule along each edge if the exponent equals the Casimir difference $\Delta C$: $\mathbf1$–$\mathbf{52}$ gives $18$, $\mathbf{52}$–$\mathbf{324}$ gives $8$, and $\mathbf{26}$–$\mathbf{273}$ gives $12$. But chapter 8 (Section `sec-tpg`, and `exr-tpg-check`) uses exponent $\Delta C/4$ for $sl_2$, where $\langle1\rangle$ comes from $\Delta C=4$. The two conventions differ by a factor of 4 in the exponent. The chapter must state which convention it uses (presumably the $q$ of the short-root normalization, $q_i=q^{\alpha_i^2/2}$, which for $U_q(e_6^{(2)})$ changes the exponents), and give the Casimir values so that the reader can check the table.

**A6. Breathers as quantum-group singlets (`20-quantum-group-symmetry.qmd`, Section `sec-qg-consequences`, bullet "Breathers"; `21-construction.qmd`, Section `sec-breather-test`; `22-known-smatrices.qmd`, bullet "non-unitarity").** The text says breathers are quantum-group singlets, so their scattering with solitons is a scalar times the identity. A singlet is a one-dimensional representation. Breathers of a theory with several species need not form one-dimensional representations; they form multiplets of the bound-state type, or they are singlets only in the sine-Gordon and $a_n$ lowest-breather cases checked here. Restate the claim as: "the lowest breathers used here are singlets, and then their scattering is scalar"; or justify the general statement.

**A7. Scalar factor as "not a change of basis" (`21-construction.qmd`, Section `sec-crossing-sign`, paragraph on $q\to-q$).** The text says the conjugation by $\Pi\otimes1$ "acts on one soliton only, it is not a change of basis". $\Pi\otimes1$ is an invertible matrix, so it is a change of basis on $V\otimes V$. What the argument needs is that it is not a change of single-particle basis of the form $A\otimes A$, which is what chapter 7 (`sec-six-vertex`) actually shows. Rewrite the sentence.

**A8. Crossing point as a power of $q$ (`21-construction.qmd`, Section `sec-an-example`, paragraph "Crossing").** It says $x(i\pi)=e^{i\pi h\omega}$ "is a power of $q$". With $q=-e^{-i\pi\omega}$ one gets $q^{-h}=(-1)^h e^{i\pi h\omega}$, so $e^{i\pi h\omega}=(-1)^hq^{-h}$. The statement holds up to the sign $(-1)^h$. Add the sign.

**A9. Fusion positions for $e_6^{(2)}$ and $c_n^{(1)}$ (`25-e6-solitons.qmd`, Section `sec-fixing-zeros-mass-pattern`).** The text says the fusions sit at $t=2T/H+T/3$ (for $\mathbf{53}$), $t=2T/H$ (for $\mathbf{299}$) and $t=2T/3$ (self-fusion), and then gives $H=2T/\omega$. The last equality uses $2T/H=\omega$, which is the $a=\omega$ zero. Make the choice of zero explicit, since the three values $a\in\{\omega,4\omega+1,6\omega+2\}$ are then the three fusion positions.

**A10. Numerical identities to recheck before citing.** Chapters 23–26 give closed forms that I did not check: the $c_3^{(1)}$ residue ranks and fusion table (`23`, Section `sec-c3-spectrum`), the $W_a(t)$ formula (`26`, Section `sec-one-loop-data-fix`), the CDD and Barnes-type exponent claims (`26`, Section `sec-implications`, and "What a correction would have to be"), and the $c_3$ breather block table (`23`, Section `sec-breather-amplitudes`). Each is marked numerical or verified in the text. Rerun the scripts named in these chapters (`soliton_rmatrix_code/`, `foundations_code/vernier/`, `foundations_code/irf/`) and record the date and output.

## B. Gaps for a reader following Parts I–V

**B1. Normalization of $\beta$ in Part VI.** Chapters 20–26 use "$\beta^2$" in at least three senses: the book normalization (longest root of length 2), the literature normalization of twisted algebras (longest root of length $2k$, so $\beta_{\rm lit}^2=\beta^2/k$), and the Vernier–Jacobsen–Saleur coupling ($\beta^2=8\gamma$). Chapter 24 says "in the normalization of [@gm1995; @dgz1992] the longest root has squared length 4, $\omega=2\pi/\beta^2-1$" and then "in our normalization $\omega=4\pi/\beta^2-1$". Put a table at the start of chapter 20: for each family, the normalization of $\beta$, the value of $\omega$ in terms of it, and the relation $1/\omega\approx\beta^2/4\pi$ or $\beta^2/2\pi$ or $\beta^2/8\pi$ that the family uses. This gap is the same as in the Part IV and V briefs.

**B2. The scalar factor is not fully defined (`21-construction.qmd`, Section `sec-scalar-factor`).** The text says each factor of $f$ is "normalized by a $t$-independent constant so that it converges", without giving the constants. A reader cannot reproduce $f$ from the text. Give the normalization, or the explicit definition of the convergent product in one line.

**B3. Lift principle and what it fixes (`21-construction.qmd`, Section `sec-integer-lifts`).** The rule is "the fusion angles must make the soliton mass ratios equal, exactly, to the particle mass ratios". Chapter 26 shows that this rule fails for $f_4^{(1)}$ at one loop. The chapter should say that the rule is an assumption that the book tests, not a theorem, and state what it fixes in each family.

**B4. Quantum dimensions and the RSOS face weights (`27-rsos.qmd`).** The statement of `prp-rsos-kinks` uses $d_h$ "the quantum dimension of the height $h$", and the Jimbo–Miwa–Okado face weight $\sqrt{[A+1][A-1]}/[A]$ is used without its definition. **B5. Breather and kink labels (`27-rsos.qmd`, Section `sec-qg-restriction`).** "Breathers carry no label" and "heights are vacua between kinks" are used before the reader has seen the IRF form. A paragraph linking the two is needed, or a forward pointer to `sec-irf` (Part II).

**B6. Infinite products and CDD-type factors (`26-f4-solitons.qmd`, Sections `sec-implications` and "half-integer conjecture").** The text uses "Barnes double-Gamma structure", "Gamma-function towers" and "CDD factor" without definitions. Give one line each, with the standard reference (Barnes 1899 or Dorey's lectures).

**B7. Which claims are proved, checked numerically, or conjectural (throughout Part VI).** Many claims are marked "[Numerical]" or "[Conjecture]", but some sentences state results as facts (for example the $c_3^{(1)}$ fusion table, the $f_4^{(1)}$ no-go, the RSOS criterion). **B8. Dependence on Part V (`21`, `23`, `26`).** The soliton masses in Part VI are taken from Part V (one-loop soliton masses, $H_s$ versus $H_b$). Give one forward reference per use, to `sec-one-loop-masses-folded` or `sec-soliton-masses-one-loop`.

## Resolution (2026-10-10)

- **A1.** No change to the criterion. The brief's claim is wrong. `prp-rsos-kinks` is about the $su(3)$ quantum dimensions $d_h=[h_1+1][h_2+1][h_1+h_2+2]/[2]$, which are invariant under $q\to-q$. The mod-$2p$ criterion holds for the $su(2)$ numbers $[k]_q$ of `exr-quantum-dims`, and that exercise already states it correctly. Checked 2026-10-10 (`qdim3.py` in the scratch directory, all $4\le p\le30$, $1\le r<2p$ coprime, 548 cases): "all $su(2)$ $[k]_q>0$" holds iff $r\equiv\pm1\pmod{2p}$, and "all level-$(p-3)$ $su(3)$ $d_h>0$" holds iff $r\equiv\pm1\pmod p$, with no exceptions in either case. The brief's example $(p,r)=(5,4)$ is $W_3(5,9)$, which has no negative $d_h$ (`foundations_code/irf/out_ha.txt`: 0/6). The eight Hermitian-analytic couplings in `out_ha.txt` are exactly those with $r\equiv\pm1\pmod p$, and $W_3(7,9)$ ($r=2$) correctly falls under part 2. Added: definitions of $[x]$, $A$ and $d_h$, with the remark on $q\to-q$, and one more clause in `exr-quantum-dims`. Side finding, not edited: `irf/checks.py` and `tm2.py` print `breathers=yes` when $3r>p$, but Takács–Watts (hep-th/9810006, eq. (3.3): breather levels $p=1,\dots,[3\lambda/2]$) give $3r>2p$, which is what the text uses. Only the printed labels of the scripts are affected (for example $W_3(5,7)$ and $W_3(8,11)$), not the text.
- **A2.** Partly right. The value $\beta^2/2\pi$ is correct in the chapter's own coupling (longest root of squared length 4, $\omega=2\pi/\beta^2-1$), but the sentence did not say which normalization it used. It now says $1/\omega=\beta^2/2\pi+O(\beta^4)$ in that coupling, which is $\beta^2/4\pi$ in the book normalization.
- **A3.** Partly right. The lifts are integer shifts: the R-matrix poles fix $\gamma\in\mathbb Z$, $\gamma'\in\frac12+\mathbb Z$ (pole at $-q^{-6}$) and $d\in\frac12+\mathbb Z$, so half-integer $\gamma'$ is forced. "Exactly two solutions" was wrong, however. The solutions are $d=\frac92+6m$, $\gamma=1+m$, $\gamma'=\frac52+3m$ for every integer $m$, all with the same one-loop shifts. Within the shift window $\{-2,\dots,2\}$ of `sec-f4-obstruction` exactly $m=0,-1$ survive, giving (a) and (b). The text now says this. The $T=6\omega+\frac32+6j$ family of `sec-soliton-masses-one-loop` was rechecked and is correct. The lift coefficients were renamed $c,c'\to\gamma,\gamma'$ (C8).
- **A4.** GMW (hep-th/9509007, PDF read) construct the $\mathbf{27}$ R-matrix of $U_q(e_6^{(2)})$ by the tensor-product graph for the horizontal subalgebra $U_q(c_4)$, under which the $\mathbf{27}$ is irreducible and its square multiplicity-free (their Sec. 4.4 and abstract). Ch. 22 now qualifies "not multiplicity-free" as "under $U_q(f_4)$", adds GMW to results item 5, and says "re-derive" in the abstract. Ch. 26 says how GMW did it, that our construction is independent, and that the two were not compared pole by pole. Its notes now read "constructed by", and "new here" now applies to the amplitude.

With Casimirs normalized so that short roots have squared length 2 ($C=52,48,36,24,0$), the rule is $\langle\Delta C/2\rangle$.
